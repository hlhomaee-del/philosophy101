Orientation Tutorial

This is not about memorizing philosophers. It is about learning how to reason with clarity, detect weak thinking, and improve arguments.

What Is Philosophy?

Philosophy is disciplined inquiry into fundamental questions about truth, reality, knowledge, value, and reasoning.

It is not random opinion-sharing.

Concept Check

Which best describes philosophy?

A disciplined method of questioning assumptions and reasoning carefully
Just sharing personal beliefs

Philosophical Questions

Philosophical questions investigate concepts and assumptions beneath ordinary claims.

Factual: “What year was the constitution signed?”
Practical: “How do we reduce traffic?”
Philosophical: “What makes a law just?”

Critical Thinking Foundations

Focus on clarity, precision, consistency, evidence, and logical connection.

Ask: What is being claimed? What supports it? What is assumed?

Arguments: Premises and Conclusions

Premise: support statement

Conclusion: what the argument tries to prove

Example: All humans are mortal. Socrates is human. Therefore, Socrates is mortal.

Identify the conclusion:

All humans are mortal
Socrates is mortal

Assumptions, Ambiguity, and Flawed Reasoning

Good thinkers detect what is left unsaid.

Hidden assumption: an unstated belief the argument depends on.

Building Better Arguments

Strengthen by clarifying claims, adding evidence, and removing vague language.

Objections and Counterarguments

A strong thinker can challenge a claim fairly and respond thoughtfully.
